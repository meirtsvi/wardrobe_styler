## 2. Top complaints, with frequency estimates

Frequency scale: VERY HIGH = appears in essentially every aggregated summary and in many independent snippets; HIGH = multiple independent quotes; MEDIUM = 2–3 quotes; LOW = single quote.

### 2.1 Hard paywall after a long onboarding (VERY HIGH — the #1 theme on both stores)
- "After going through all the setup, including providing an email and a selfie, users learned that there is an immediate paywall that does not allow you to see ANY features within the app" (App Store)
- "an immediate paywall that doesn't allow users to see any features within the app or explore the layout before purchasing"
- "every option labeled 'pro' and users getting hit with a paywall immediately upon opening the app"
- "There's an option that says 'continue with limited features' and instead of giving you a free version; it sends you to another option to pay $1.66 a month and they claim it's 80% off from $55" (App Store) — i.e. the fake-out "limited features" button is itself a second paywall.
- "the app's subscription model being revealed only at the end of the sign-up process after users have invested time setting up their wardrobe" (2026 review)
- Google Play summary: "The major drawback across reviews is the paywall and lack of a free option, including mentions of trials requiring a credit card and general dissatisfaction with pricing." "there's a pro version but that seems like a cash grab"
- justuseapp summary: "A pervasive issue is the aggressive paywall, which often appears only after a lengthy onboarding process, leading to frustration and feelings of deception."
- Developer stance (reply): "Essembl is designed as a premium experience and the app clearly shows before activation that a subscription is required"; "Essembl is a paid experience with a free trial available on the annual plan, and this is shown before activation on the paywall and in the confirmation."
- screensdesign (design site) describes it approvingly as the growth tactic: "hard paywall with a free trial offer presented after a lengthy and engaging onboarding process. At 03:22, the user is prompted to start a 3-day free trial, after which they will be charged for a yearly or weekly plan. The yearly plan is highlighted as 'Most Popular' and its price is broken down into a monthly equivalent... By gating the entire app experience behind this paywall, Essembl ensures that only highly motivated users who have completed the personalization flow convert."
- Sources: apps.apple.com reviews; play.google.com summary via appbrain; justuseapp; screensdesign.com/showcase/essembl-ai-styling-assistant

## 4. AI quality issues (consolidated)

1. **Regenerated vs. real garments** — pipeline appears to be: detect+describe (good) -> generate a synthetic "clean" image of a *similar* item (bad: colour shifts, changed cut, hallucinated details, "different vibe"). Users interpret this as "the app replaced my clothes". Confidence high (multiple independent quotes + dev reply admitting colour/detail issues and promising real cut-outs).
2. **Colour drift** — "change the colour slightly"; colour-match judgments wrong; outfit check claims colours clash.
3. **Mis-categorisation** — dresses as tops; jackets in tops; no one-piece class; cropping whole photo as one item; "capturing entire photos".
4. **Combination logic** — two jackets; dress + trousers; formal occasion with wrong shoes; cowboy boots only with shorts/joggers; ignores the anchor item the user picked.
5. **Coverage/variety** — uses a small subset of wardrobe; same outfits daily; fixates on one accessory.
6. **Context** — weather wrong for location; occasion not respected.
7. **Style feedback** — outfit check either always negative or (per competitor) sycophantic; no explanation grounded in colour theory/proportion; no body analysis.
8. **Personalisation** — "doesn't fully understand your aesthetic"; "claims to learn" but doesn't visibly adapt; colour analysis produces a report but doesn't feed the generator.

- Onboarding requires **email + selfie before the paywall**; reviewers list this as part of the "deception" complaint (data collected before the price is disclosed). Source: App Store reviews via justuseapp/appshunter.

No review explicitly complains about photo privacy, but the selfie-before-paywall pattern and the 33.4/100 justuseapp score are the trust signals people see when they search "is Essembl legit". Confidence medium.

- Billing trust: charged after cancel; trial-to-$80 surprise; refunds only after emailing.
- Marketing trust: "falsely advertising the AI feature"; "marketing and onboarding need to be much clearer about what the app actually does".

## 7. UI/UX flow details (from screensdesign + reviews)

- **Onboarding**: long personalised quiz (style, occasions, etc.), collects email, asks for a selfie (used for colour analysis / outfit check), then hard paywall at roughly the 3:22 mark of the recorded flow. Paywall: yearly highlighted "Most Popular", price shown as monthly equivalent, 3-day trial CTA; alternative weekly plan; declining shows a second "continue with limited features" screen that is actually a discounted annual offer (~$1.66/mo, "80% off").
- **Home**: daily suggested outfits ("Daily outfit" widget exists); suggested outfits persist on home and cannot be deleted; July-2025 redesign of homescreen.
- **Wardrobe add**: photo of pile/flat lay -> AI detects items -> shows generated white-background renders -> user adds to wardrobe; batch limited to ~10 items per run (per review); items rendered as synthetic look-alikes; "select clothes from handpicked items" starter catalogue added in 2026 to reduce cold start.
- **Outfit generator**: choose occasion/mood/weather (geolocation opt-in), optionally anchor an item; results are top/bottom/shoes slots (no layering slot; one top only); "new design of the outfit generator" teased on TikTok mid-2024.
- **Outfit Check**: upload/selfie of an outfit -> score + granular feedback across Color, Fit, Texture; "preview of outfit check change recommendations" (visual upgrade) added; daily streaks gamification.
- **Find items / shop**: upload screenshot -> identify items -> where to buy; "Shopping Buddy" chat (0.2.27).
- **Chat with AI stylist**: free-text occasion -> outfit ideas.
- **Support**: no in-app support path evident; reviewers had to hunt for an email; contact@essembl.com.

## 8. Prioritised "Do better" list for a competing iOS app

Ordered by (frequency x severity in Essembl feedback) x (feasibility for a new app).

### P0 — Trust & monetisation (fixes the #1 and #6 complaints)
1. **Let people see and use the product before any paywall.** Free tier that includes: unlimited (or generous, e.g. 75–100 item) wardrobe, N outfit generations/week, browsing the whole UI. Put the paywall on *volume/advanced* features (unlimited generations, packing lists, try-on), never on "see the app". Essembl's own dev replies show they will not do this; it is the cheapest differentiation available.
2. **No selfie or email before value.** Sign-in optional (Sign in with Apple only when the user wants sync). Ask for a selfie only at the moment a colour-analysis feature needs it, with an explicit "used only for X, stored on device / deletable" note.
3. **Honest pricing UI.** One real price, no "80% off from $55" anchor, no fake "limited features" button. Show the trial end date on the paywall and send a local notification 24h before the trial converts (this directly answers the "$80 with no reminder" complaint). Offer in-app "cancel/manage subscription" deep link and an in-app refund request path.
4. **In-app support & status.** Support form inside Settings with device/app logs attached; visible "known issues" banner when a backend outage (e.g. outfits not loading) is happening; target reply < 24h. Essembl's silence for a week on a paying user is a recurring 1-star trigger.

### P0 — The wardrobe must be the user's real clothes
5. **Segment and cut out the actual garment from the user's photo; never replace it with a generated look-alike.** Use on-device/VisionKit-style subject lifting or a segmentation model, keep the original pixels, optionally offer a "clean-up" (shadow removal, background white) that is clearly *edit*, not *regenerate*. Show original vs. cut-out side by side and let the user pick. This is the single most damaging Essembl review ("Thanks, I understand what you own. Now let me show you something else.").
6. **Colour fidelity**: extract dominant colours from the original photo pixels (not from the generated render); show the swatch and let the user correct it in one tap.
7. **Editable metadata with confidence**: show detected category/colour/pattern/season tags with an "edit" affordance on the confirmation screen; flag low-confidence detections for review instead of silently committing.
8. **Correct taxonomy from day one**: one-piece (dress/romper/jumpsuit), tops, mid-layers, outerwear, bottoms, shoes, bags, jewellery, accessories. Rules: one-piece excludes bottoms; outerwear is a layer *over* a top; allow multiple layers (tank + shirt + jacket).
9. **Bulk ingestion that runs in the background**: accept 50+ photos at once, process server-side, notify when done, let the user review/approve in a queue. No "10 items at a time".
10. **Never lose data**: iCloud/CloudKit sync tied to Apple ID, local cache, export (ZIP of images + CSV/JSON). Address the "lost account access with Apple sign-in" fear explicitly.

### P1 — Make the generator obviously better
11. **Constraint-respecting generation**: if the user anchors an item, it must appear; enforce occasion/dress-code rules (no sneakers with black tie unless "fashion-forward" toggle), no duplicate slots (two jackets), weather from CoreLocation + WeatherKit with the temperature shown on the card so users can see it was considered.
12. **Coverage & variety**: track per-item "last suggested" and wear counts; explicitly rotate; show "you haven't worn these 12 items — want outfits with them?" Fixes "same outfits every day" and "only one pair of earrings out of 15".
13. **Explain every outfit**: 1–2 line rationale (colour harmony rule used, silhouette balance, weather). Turns "random" into "styled" even when the combination is similar. Also fixes the Outfit-Check complaint ("always says it looks bad" with no reason).
14. **Per-item feedback on suggestions**: thumbs-down on a *slot* (not the whole outfit) with reasons (colour, too formal, don't like this item, wrong season) that actually update preferences; show "learned" chips so users see personalisation happening.
15. **Manual outfit builder / canvas** ("lay in bed and move things in and out"): drag-and-drop from wardrobe, save, duplicate, calendar-assign. Also "swap this piece" on any AI outfit.
16. **Style references**: let users upload/paste Pinterest or screenshot inspiration; extract palette/silhouette/vibe and use it as a generation conditioning signal. Directly requested and dev-acknowledged but not shipped at Essembl.
17. **Connect colour analysis to generation**: if a seasonal palette is computed, filter/boost items by palette and label outfits "in your palette". Warn about white-balance and offer a grey-card / daylight tip before the selfie.
18. **Delete/hide/snooze suggested outfits** on Home; nothing persistent that the user can't remove.

### P2 — Differentiators Essembl is criticised for lacking
19. Body-shape/proportion-aware advice (optional profile: height, fit preferences) and gendered style guidance where relevant (mensfashioner critique).
20. Calendar/planning + packing lists (absent in Essembl; standard in Stylebook/WardrobeX — competitor roundups mention).
21. Duplicate detection at import ("do you already own this?") and cost-per-wear / wear-count (Whering/Indyx strengths; Essembl has none).
22. Transparent "AI honesty" mode for Outfit Check: calibrated score, consistent across resubmits, with what to change and a preview (Essembl added preview but the score is perceived as arbitrary).
23. iPad/large-screen and widget support that actually works (Essembl has an iPad build and a daily-outfit widget, both with loading complaints).

### Positioning copy to steal from the complaints
- "Your real clothes, not AI look-alikes."
- "See everything before you pay. No selfie, no card to start."
- "Outfits that explain themselves."
- "Never the same outfit twice unless you ask."
